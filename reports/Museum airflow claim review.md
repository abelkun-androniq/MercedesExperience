## Give the splitter one pressure datum and measure its actual load

The current code overwrites all nine inlet populations with equilibrium every step after streaming. This fixes density, velocity and the nonequilibrium stress at that column. The outlet copies all nine populations from its interior neighbor. The inlet therefore already pins a density value, but the calculation lacks a controlled downstream static pressure. In the saved coarse means, just three cells downstream, the column-mean axial speed is **0.855 U** in both settings. The mean pressure has risen about **2.69 dynamic pressures**, with a vertical range of roughly one dynamic pressure. Column three also moves physically when the grid changes. It is a poor freestream reference. ([Boundary code](../lib/model/flow.js#L53), [coarse field audit](<../research_notes/Museum airflow claim review/section.md>))

For the replacement, prescribe inlet normal speed U and zero transverse speed. After streaming, retain the known populations and reconstruct the missing incoming populations with a Zou–He velocity boundary, solving for density. At a remote fluid outlet prescribe one static density, for example 1, and reconstruct the normal velocity with a consistent tangential closure. Treat corners at the road, ceiling and solids explicitly. Choose an outlet beyond sustained reverse flow. A sponge confined to the downstream buffer is useful if a pressure pulse or wake reflects back into the probes. Establish the datum and verify flux balance first. Then move or remove the sponge and check the measured effect. ([Zou and He](https://arxiv.org/abs/comp-gas/9508001), [Junk and Yang on outflow](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/communications-in-computational-physics/article/abs/asymptotic-analysis-of-lattice-boltzmann-outflow-treatments/1BB76D8897738AE6999B52378EB35761), [Xu and Sagaut on absorbing layers](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6350))

The outlet sets a gauge. For a freestream coefficient use an upstream patch at fixed physical coordinates, verify a plateau in density and speed across multiple heights and stations, and use its static pressure and dynamic pressure consistently. In lattice units, \(C_p=c_s^2(\rho-\rho_\infty)/(\tfrac12\rho_\infty U_\infty^2)\), with \(c_s^2=1/3\). A value above one alone does not diagnose a solver error. One is the ideal incompressible stagnation result under specific upstream conditions. The observed inlet-layer variation is more informative. At U = 0.1, a rise of 2–4 nominal dynamic pressures corresponds to a substantial 3–6% density rise. Monitor **peak local Mach** and density range when changing the box. ([Isentropic relations](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/BGH/isentrop.html))

**The cause of the drift remains open.** A spatially uniform pressure offset cancels from a simultaneous upper-minus-lower difference. Its changing value implicates the developing flow or a spatially varying boundary error. For a straight laminar gap of height \(g=0.12\) m, the slowest viscous startup mode takes \(g^2/(\pi^2\nu)\) to decay by a factor of e. With the specified molecular Re this is **5.6 original tunnel transits**. An acoustic crossing takes about 0.17 transit. The open, developing channel can adjust its velocity profile and flux split over many passes. Eddy viscosity and entrance flow change this estimate. Three settling transits distinguish neither mechanism. ([Parallel-plate startup solution, §4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.1037))

First run an empty uniform tunnel with the road moving at U, then a laminar moving-road/fixed-ceiling channel with a compatible analytical profile and closure switched off. For the actual nose, record fluid mass, inlet/outlet mass flux, upper and gap fluxes, upstream and underfloor pressure, and the desired force on the same time base. Check the mass change against net flux over each interval. A drifting inventory, a stable inventory with changing branch split, and reflected disturbances call for different fixes. For the failed 560 mm step, record where the first invalid value appears, local speed and outlet backflow. A sharp expansion creates its own separation problem. The NaN cannot identify the cause of the original drift. ([Couette benchmark, §3.5](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-24190.pdf))

**Correct the observable before buying another fine run.** The retracted leading edge is at x = 935 mm and the forward edge at 975 mm. Both settings use pressure boxes over x = 940–970 mm. Only the extended setting has a lip between those boxes. For horizontal wetted faces the downward pressure force per unit span is \(F'_{\rm down}=\int(p_{\rm upper}-p_{\rm lower})\,dx\). A chord-average times the chord represents that patch's load when both faces exist and wall pressure is estimated consistently. The new exposed strip contributes zero exposed area in the retracted setting. For a genuine two-state comparison, use vertical force on the wetted forebody through the same downstream station in both geometries. Integrate surface pressure with its vertical normal and check against wall momentum exchange, which also includes viscous force. Use one fixed force normalization. The project labels travel and thickness illustrative and gives a nose trace uncertainty of roughly 100 mm. ([Probe and polygons](../tools/cfd/part.mjs#L94), [geometry assumptions](../stops/190e-evo2/car.js#L129), [surface pressure force](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/aerodynamic-force/))

## Let both flow paths rejoin or specify what drives them

A **finite 2-D section** with a plausible underfloor, closed tail and a downstream wake is the simplest reference for the car interpretation. Both paths then discharge into the same exterior before the outlet. A truncated nose can answer a **rig** question at lower cost. Its gap length and its upper/lower exit pressures or downstream resistance must be part of the specification. Equal exit pressures at a cut through the body impose a particular flow split. For a vehicle interpretation, take the branch pressure difference or boundary data from a coarser finite parent, then vary the cut position and bracket the back pressure. Allow normal flow to respond.

The true maximum solid height of the nose is 744 − 120 = 624 mm. That gives **25% solid blockage** at H = 2.5 m and 18% at H = 3.5 m. The quoted 30% includes the open ground gap. Even a 10% geometric target requires H = 6.24 m for this nose. No height or upstream distance alone bounds the error in the *difference between settings*. First test both lip positions on the coarse grid in the existing enlargement, from 1 to 2 m upstream, 1.6 to 2.4 m downstream of the bumper, and 2.5 to 3.5 m high. Compare the two case contrasts. If sensitive, vary the ceiling, inlet and outlet separately. For a finite parent, start several body heights upstream and downstream of the vehicle and verify by enlargement, checking reference uniformity and outlet recovery. The present exporter checks only the retracted splitter in the larger box. ([Domain and exporter](../tools/cfd/part.mjs#L92), [vehicle-domain sensitivity study, §4.3](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-8921/2/2/18))

## Spend the fine-grid hours on a contrast that survives the coarse tests

Prespecify the signed observable, physical support, a material tolerance, and the production horizon after a coarse pilot. Save short-interval scalar histories or block sums on the probe region. Follow mass, gap flux and each case's observable through startup. Compare separated early and late production segments and check sensitivity to discarding the first segment. Require the uncertainty band on the early-to-late shift to fit inside the material tolerance. An insignificant slope with a wide interval leaves drift unresolved. The observed coarse contrasts **0.990, 0.849, 0.744** average 0.861 and pass the current endpoint-drift comparison despite decreasing in every window. ([Current rule and preview](../tools/cfd/part.mjs#L28), [splitter record](../tools/cfd/results/splitter.json))

From the plateau series estimate the integral correlation time for the local observable. Choose nonoverlapping batches several correlation times long, check that neighboring batch means are weakly correlated, and check uncertainty after doubling block length. Aim for roughly **8–12 effectively independent batches** as a practical starting point. For separate stationary runs and a linear mean, use a Welch interval for \(\Delta=\bar B-\bar A\) with standard error \(\sqrt{s_A^2/K_A+s_B^2/K_B}\) from the batch sample variances. Simultaneous step numbers do not pair independent eddies. For an angle, threshold width or streamline of a mean field, resample whole decorrelated blocks of velocity-component sums, rebuild the pooled mean field, and recompute the observable. ([Oliver et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0828), [Rezaeiravesh et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08676))

Require a prespecified interval, for example 95%, wholly on the same side of zero **on each grid**. On the coarse grid, enlarge the domain for **both** settings. Bound the resulting change of contrasts, including sampling uncertainty, below a prespecified fraction of the smaller credible effect, for example one third. Check comparable physical averaging durations and plausible geometry/probe perturbations. Two passing grids support a sign only for the tested model. Stop at the budget cap with an inconclusive sign if slow adjustment or sampling error still spans the decision margin.

At the stated throughput, an original splitter transit per state costs about **1.4 minutes at 5 mm and 11.3 minutes at 2.5 mm**. Six fine transits for two states already use **2.25 machine-hours** before overhead. The coarse log actually took about 3.4 minutes per averaging window. A uniform 10 × 8 m fine outer domain would take about nine hours *per transit*. Spend the first hours on force tagging, boundary benchmarks and paired coarse sensitivity. Use a calibrated fine local section only if those pass. ([Timing and calculations](<../research_notes/Museum airflow claim review/statistics.md>))

## The wheel and wing need different kinds of evidence

At fixed physical Re, hold geometry, gap/diameter, rotation ratio, ground speed, boundary locations and data, lattice Mach, closure coefficient, physical probe and averaging duration consistent. At U = 0.08 the two molecular relaxation times are about 0.50068 and 0.50102. The local modeled viscosity scales as \((C_s\Delta)^2|S|\). Near-half molecular relaxation alone cannot quantify the shares of molecular, modeled and numerical dissipation. Log local effective relaxation, eddy/molecular viscosity ratio, peak Mach and voxelized gaps near the tyre and shear layer. A two-grid change with a grid-tied filter is a robustness check of the combined model and discretization. To isolate discretization error in one filtered model would require a fixed *physical* explicit filter and consistent closure. Tuning a global “effective Re” cannot accomplish that. ([Collision rule](../tools/cfd/lbm3d.mjs#L89), [Bose, Moin and You](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3485774))

**W 196 R.** The quoted millimetres are lateral **edge coordinates**. At 24 mm the three widths are **220, 232, 232 mm exposed** and **44, 40, 40 mm enclosed**. Fine enclosed gives 24, 32, 32 mm. The rule thresholds the magnitude of the *mean velocity vector*. In the enclosed case the inner “edge” is the first point clear of the interpolation mask against the flank. Its speed is only 0.21 U on the coarse grid. The narrow band is roughly two cells wide. Reprocessing the saved coarse means preserved the ordering at three cutoffs (0.7–0.9 U) and three stations (0.6–0.8 m behind the axle), but the enclosed band and exposed tyre wake occupy different regions. Projected frontal blockage also differs, about **21% enclosed versus 10% exposed**. The shapes change nose and fender as well as wheel cover. ([Band selection](../tools/cfd/part.mjs#L186), [wheel and field audit](<../research_notes/Museum airflow claim review/parts.md>))

Prefer the mean streamwise velocity deficit integrated over a fixed physical patch in fluid common to both bodies, after verifying it captures both features. Otherwise measure farther downstream of both finite bodies, or ask a wheel-shielding question and accumulate streamwise force on the tagged tyre. A local deficit alone cannot yield vehicle drag. The still W 196 tyre spans 6–9 cells across and has a clipped bottom with a 48 mm air passage, only 2–3 cells deep. Rotation and contact geometry affect wake and separation. Test both bodies in the larger coarse box and bracket the wheel treatment before attributing the result to a rolling car. The existing queue includes only exposed-large. ([Geometry](../tools/cfd/corner.mjs#L19), [wheel rotation and contact study](https://digitalcommons.calpoly.edu/aero_fac/151/))

**Evo arch.** The published line follows the horizontal projection of the mean field at hub height. At 600 mm behind the axle its full-mean displacement is **372 mm outward**. The 24 mm windows give 367, 374 and 377 mm. Neighboring seeds also turn outward in the saved field. This is a streamline illustration from a specified seed. A moving air parcel would require time-resolved velocities. A hypothetical open/closed wheelhouse would answer a different venting question and needs resolved geometry of its own. Keep the single fitted shape. ([Arch record](../tools/cfd/results/evo-arch.json), [trace definition](../tools/cfd/volume.mjs#L62))

**Evo wing.** Raising the flap changes the sampled angle by **+12.6° at 28 mm and +8.0° at 40 mm**. Moving the box 40 mm toward the centreline on the coarse mesh gives +2.1° to +5.0°: the increase survives while the absolute downward-to-upward crossing disappears. The original boxes contain only 48 and 12 fluid cells. Each stored mean spans roughly 1.2 or 1.7 tunnel transits respectively, with no blockwise wing-angle history. First extend the existing whole-car run with block sums of the local velocity components. A local solve becomes worthwhile for a better surveyed flap and a new target such as separation structure. It would need upstream boundary-layer profile, downwash and fluctuations from the parent, compatible flux and pressure boundaries, and reproduction of the parent baseline in the overlap. A finer crop of the game mesh with a steady mean inlet cannot establish road-car downforce. ([Wing record](../tools/cfd/results/wing.json), [averaging procedure](../tools/cfd/run.mjs#L30), [probe sensitivity](<../research_notes/Museum airflow claim review/parts.md>))

For the museum, place the wing's sampling box and the arch's seed directly on their images. That lets visitors see precisely where each statement applies. Reserve historical force, horsepower or drag figures for independent evidence about the actual car.
