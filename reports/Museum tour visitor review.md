# Give visitors a reason to look twice

**Visitor review · 26 November 2026 · local revision `d868721`**

At the 300 SL, a visitor can look up at the gullwing door, lower the sides of its virtual frame, and see the structure lose roughly two-thirds of its stiffness in the model. That is the tour at its best. The room picker and three-stop highlights now make that experience easier to reach. **The first priority is trustworthy wayfinding** — two published exhibit locations conflict with the route, and the link back from a stop loses the visitor’s gallery. The strongest content addition would be one verified Legend 6 exhibit that explains emission-free propulsion. For visual impact, some of the best material is already built and waiting to be discovered. [300 SL experiment](../stops/300sl/car.js) · [Walking route](../stops/tour.js)

## Eleven galleries cover the building unevenly

**46 stops cover all seven Legend rooms and Collections 1–4.** Legend 1 alone has eight stops. Legend 6 has four, covering suspension and combustion, a folding roof, crash structure, and escape hinges. A visitor could complete that gallery and miss its account of the transition to emission-free mobility entirely. Pick one object confirmed on display and let visitors trace energy from its source to the wheels. If that vehicle supports regeneration, reverse the trace under braking. Collection 5’s changing exhibitions and the Level 0 technology area also merit short, dated directions at the appropriate point in the walk. [Tour inventory](../stops/tour.js) · [Museum exhibition guide, pp. 3–4](https://www.mercedes-benz.com/content/dam/brandhub/assets/art-and-culture/mercedes-benz-museum/museum/Media-Information-Mercedes-Benz-Museum-EN.pdf) · [Museum’s Legend 6 description](https://www.mercedes-benz.com/en/art-and-culture/museum/exhibition/legend-6/)

**Verify two locations before directing another visitor there with confidence.** The tour places the Blitzen-Benz, one of its three highlights, in Legend 2. Stuttgart’s tourism office puts it on the banked racing curve, which the museum identifies with Legend 7. The O 302 appears under Collection 1 with an uncertainty marker. Daimler Truck’s November 2022 photographs place the World Cup bus in Collection 4. Those photos also show two excellent identification cues, the fan by the front door and the Tip and Tap mascots at the rear. Obtain a dated floor or curator check, carry the result through the picker, route order, stop header and highlights, then try finding each vehicle from its gallery entrance. Until then, show visitors which placement was last documented and when. ## One good question unlocks an existing model

The route has **64 component entries using 24 subsystem types**. Nineteen entries have a dedicated prompt, 17 have an answer, and 18 include a real-part illustration. Other entries teach through controls and prose, so these figures measure editorial structure. The LP 333 shows where a small edit could pay off. Its twin-steer model already displays individual wheel angles, slip and turning radius. Ask visitors to hold the first axle at 20°, predict which tyres will scrub, then fix the second axle straight. The reveal is the second pair sliding sideways because they point away from their rolling paths around the turn. A visitor who can point to those tyres and explain the scrub has understood more than a slip-angle readout alone conveys. [Inventory method](<../research_notes/Museum tour visitor review/engineering.md>) · [LP 333](../stops/lp333/car.js) · [Twin-steer controls](../subsystems/twin-steer.js)

Two answers need tighter bounds. The 300 SLR’s lift graph and moving rocker show exactly when the spring valve loses contact. Saying the second cam closes its valve “at any speed” extends the claim beyond the displayed experiment. Write “The second cam pulls the valve shut throughout the speed range shown.” The Evo II wing answer gives about 34 kg extra rear-axle load and 9 kW extra drag power at 200 km/h. Grid refinement affects the magnitude. Lead with what held up across runs — rear load and drag power both increased — and place the figures beside that qualification. Retest by asking visitors which result they would expect on the real car and which numbers came from this simulation. ## The most memorable details reward a closer look

**Best visual reveal:** the Evo II wheel-arch tracer. Follow air headed for the tyre and watch it turn outward past the door. The final stop contains this striking demonstration, far from the route entrance and outside the three highlights. Feature a direct invitation, “Follow the air around a racing tyre,” that lands on the experiment. Test whether a first-time visitor can follow the same stream before and after it passes the wheel. A quieter technical Easter egg is the Gullwing crankshaft’s 30° step control. Walking through the rotation lets visitors discover the mirrored piston pairs and see their shaking forces cancel. [Evo II tracer](../stops/190e-evo2/car.js) · [Gullwing engine balance](../stops/300sl/car.js)

**Best opportunity for visual polish:** the 300 SLR closing rocker. The visitor can already swap the animation with an enlarged photograph of a real Ducati rocker, identified as the donor. The photo has an accessible button name and a desktop zoom cursor, but the touch caption begins “In real life” and the visible swap instruction appears on the return journey. Add a visible “Enlarge part photo” cue. Mark the closing fork in the same colour and position where feasible in both views, with a stationary marker for reduced motion. Hand someone the phone and ask them to find what pulls the valve shut. See whether they enlarge the picture unaided and can point to the fork in each view. ## What to validate on the next museum visit

Begin in a gallery with a shared stop link. Can someone identify the exhibit from its photograph, reach the adjacent cars, and find one feature on the physical vehicle that the model helped them understand? Run that short task at the disputed Blitzen-Benz location, the LP 333 and the 300 SLR. It tests navigation, historical placement and learning in the setting where all three matter together.

This review combined a routed-content inventory and published museum material with local WebKit walks in reduced motion on emulated iPhone and landscape iPad profiles. The English and Hungarian checks sampled particular journeys. Current floor placement, physical-device behavior, German interaction, VoiceOver, offline use and first-time visitor comprehension remain for a separate field or device check.
