The five links
    What to say at the car
    • Five rods, each loaded mostly along its own axis, locate the wheel carrier. The 190 was the first series car with this axle, in 1982.
    • Watch the orange trace. Over the bump tyre load spikes; in the pothole it drops toward zero. Grip comes from that load, so a flat line is the goal.
    • The body barely moves while the wheel does the work. That is the sprung versus unsprung mass split.
    Four-stroke cycle · firing order 1-3-4-2

    What to say at the car
    • The 190 E's 2.0-litre M102: four cylinders in line, one overhead camshaft, cylinder 1 at the front.
    • Firing order 1-3-4-2 gives one power stroke every 180° of crank, which keeps the crank torque even. Watch the orange flashes walk along the block.
    • The block leans to the right so it fits under the 190's low bonnet line.

    Geometry is illustrative: link hardpoints, engine dimensions and suspension rates are plausible values, not factory data.

    Proposed tour workflow

    1. Source the model

      Download a CC model from Sketchfab as .glb, or a GrabCAD STEP and tessellate it in FreeCAD or Fusion. Build the missing ones, like this axle, from hardpoints.

    2. Prepare in Blender

      Decimate to under 50k faces, colour the parts by function, keyframe the motion. Export .glb and .usdz.

    3. One page per stop

      A static page per car with Google's <model-viewer>: orbit in the browser, and on iPhone an AR button opens Quick Look. Host free on GitHub Pages.

    4. Print the cards

      Front: the car and three talking points. Back: a QR code to that stop's page. Scan, look, no app to install.
