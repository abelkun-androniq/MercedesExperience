# Splitter boundary conditions and observable

## What sets the pressure and could cause the drift?

### Takeaway
Replace the full-population equilibrium overwrite and full-column copy with complementary low-Mach velocity-inlet and static-pressure-outlet reconstruction. That fixes a pressure datum and allows the mass flux to adjust. A time series of mass, gap flow and differential force is needed to verify whether the remaining transient is small.

### Cited Findings

- The solver first push-streams the post-collision populations, overwrites all nine populations at the inlet with a uniform equilibrium, copies all nine post-stream populations from the penultimate column into the outlet, and then swaps buffers. The reference for plotted pressure is the vertical mean of the pre-collision density moments at column 3, so its physical distance from the boundary also changes with refinement. [Boundary implementation](../../lib/model/flow.js#L53)
- The archived 5 mm result and the raw 5 mm results agree. The across-box difference in differences is +0.990, +0.849, +0.744 dynamic pressures. Both fine-grid logs showed settling only when inspected. [Archived coarse preview](../../tools/cfd/results/splitter.json) [Raw run metadata](../../tools/cfd/out/splitter/in-5.json) [Raw forward metadata](../../tools/cfd/out/splitter/forward-5.json)
- Calculated from the raw time-mean density and axial-velocity arrays, averaging all fluid cells across each sampling column. At the retracted/forward inlet itself, speed is exactly U. Three cells in, mean speed is 0.855 U in both cases, mean pressure relative to inlet density is +2.696/+2.687 q, and top-to-bottom range (max minus min) is 1.002/1.001 q. The averages combine the three drifting windows. [Retracted coarse arrays](../../tools/cfd/out/splitter/in-5.bin) [Forward coarse arrays](../../tools/cfd/out/splitter/forward-5.bin)
- Zou and He derive separate velocity and pressure rules. After streaming, retain known populations and reconstruct only missing incoming populations. Specified inlet velocity determines density by consistency; specified outlet density leaves the normal velocity to be reconstructed and needs a suitable tangential closure. Their examples are tests of the reconstruction in tractable flows. [Zou and He](https://arxiv.org/abs/comp-gas/9508001)
- The macroscopic condition produced by a population extrapolation depends on the particular rule. Sponge layers damp disturbances toward a reference state and introduce extra terms. Boundary treatment and its stability depend on the design. [Junk and Yang](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/communications-in-computational-physics/article/abs/asymptotic-analysis-of-lattice-boltzmann-outflow-treatments/1BB76D8897738AE6999B52378EB35761) [Xu and Sagaut](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6350)
- The two-plate transient provides a test case with a moving lower wall and a stationary upper wall, a diffusion equation, and a linear steady profile. [PNNL benchmark, §3.5](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-24190.pdf)

### Inferences

- Both boundary error and slow adjustment of the velocity profile/upper–lower flow split are plausible. The inlet already fixes a density at the boundary row. A uniform interior pressure offset cancels from an upper/lower difference taken at the same time, so change of that difference rules out *pure gauge shift alone*. It does not distinguish boundary interference, shear diffusion or longer unsteadiness. A gap open at both ends adjusts its throughflow. It cannot accumulate incompressible fluid indefinitely. In weakly compressible LBM its density can transiently change or boundary mass error can accrue.
- Prescribe `(u_s,u_z)=(U,0)` on the inlet, let density follow from streamed populations. On a far, primarily outward-flowing downstream cross-section prescribe a single static gauge density `rho_out=1` on fluid nodes, solve for normal velocity, and use a consistent tangential condition, e.g. zero for a truly parallel recovered outflow or tangential velocity extrapolated from the interior. Handle intersections with moving road, top, and any walls consistently and never reconstruct in solids. Ramp inlet and road together if starting from rest. A pressure outlet removes a free back-pressure choice and provides a datum, but a uniform pressure imposed across a near wake or two separated outlet paths may change the physics. A sponge is optional if the downstream signals reveal reflection. It has to be downstream of the test region and its removal or movement should have negligible effect on the metric.
- Define `Cp=(rho-rho_ref)c_s²/(0.5 rho_ref U_ref²)` in lattice units (`c_s²=1/3`). Measure `rho_ref,U_ref` over a fixed physical upstream patch with demonstrably uniform, near-freestream flow; verify at more than one station/height and on the larger domain. A remote pressure outlet defines a gauge, and a remote undisturbed upstream reference defines freestream static pressure. These values can differ in a confined, driven tunnel. The observations +2–4 q correspond to density changes 3–6% for the current U and nominal rho, already substantial. `Cp=1` is an ideal incompressible stagnation result with specified upstream total head. For ideal low-Mach isentropic or isothermal stagnation, finite Mach changes this by only approximately Ma²/4, i.e. +0.0075 at these parameters. The reference spread of about 1 q and measured inlet-layer velocity mismatch are much more diagnostic than crossing 1. [NASA relations](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/BGH/isentrop.html)
- Cheap boundary checks: run an empty tunnel with matched inflow/moving ground and uniform initial velocity (`u=U, rho=1` everywhere should persist); test a laminar moving/fixed-wall channel, with a compatible inlet profile, known force/pressure gradient and `Cs=0` for the analytic comparison. In downstream coordinate s and gap g, the fully developed volume flux per span satisfies `Q/g=U/2−g²(dp/ds)/(12μ)`. In the production model log fluid-cell mass, actual boundary link fluxes or an interior control-volume mass balance, upper and lower branch flux histories, fixed-location pressure probes, diagnostic Δp/force, min/max density, peak local Mach and outlet backflow. Separate a numerical conservation residual from physical dM/dt during startup. A sustained inventory drift or a continuing redistribution between the branches blocks release even with balanced total flux. For a NaN, locate the first invalid/population-growth site and time relative to outlet backflow and high local speed, then check on coarse grid with a farther outlet. A 560-mm step is a new separated expansion; its numerical failure is not evidence a finite tail is invalid.
- As a time-scale check at `Re=10⁴` based on `Lref=1 m`, the slowest laminar parallel-gap perturbation decays as `exp(−π²νt/g²)`. For `g=0.12 m` and `T=2.6 m/U`, the e-folding time is `5.61 T` (`g²/ν≈55.4 T`). The acoustic length crossing is `Ma T≈0.173 T`. Convective flushing, entry growth and eddy viscosity change the gap time. These estimates explain why three passes are inconclusive and set cheap monitoring intervals, not a mandatory runtime or attribution. Lower lattice U can probe compressibility but at fixed physical Re and grid lowers `τ−0.5` proportionally, so verify stability and use local Mach.

### Gaps

- Time-resolved mass and branch flux histories, revised boundary runs, and late-window forces are absent from the current records. The assigned source of the drift is unresolved.

## What domain is adequate within the budget?

### Takeaway
For a section meant to represent the vehicle, close a plausible body including underfloor, tail and wake so the two branches rejoin before one remote outlet. The cheapest defensible local alternative is an explicitly idealized nose/channel with documented downstream resistance/pressures or boundary data from a coarser full section and sensitivity to these conditions.

### Cited Findings

- The profile extends beyond the downstream plane. The regular domain has 1.0 m in front, 1.6 m behind the bumper and H=2.5 m. The larger geometry sets 2.0 m ahead, 2.4 m behind, H=3.5 m. Its standard sequence and export add only the retracted larger case. [Section setup](../../tools/cfd/part.mjs#L92) [Export](../../tools/cfd/part.mjs#L129)
- Primary vehicle-domain work expanded tunnel dimensions, measured reference pressure and then recomputed the vehicle. Gradients and integrated forces changed with confinement. Numerical sizes in those 3-D experiments do not establish 2-D splitter distances. [Automotive tunnel study §4.3](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-8921/2/2/18)

### Inferences

- At the highest model section `B=(744−120)/2500=0.2496` is solid blockage. `744/2500=0.298` is the height envelope including the open ground gap. Even steady incompressible continuity alone implies section-averaged channel speed ≈1.33× the upstream bulk speed at B=25%. The 3.5 m high domain still has B≈17.8%. This demonstrates sensitivity risk, not a correction formula for load. Geometric targets of B=10% and 5% correspond to H=6.24 and 12.48 m for this *nose*; recompute height for a whole-car section with roof. No upstream or ceiling distance certifies a small signed error.
- Practical first sensitivity at fixed 5 mm: both lip states at current and already-coded larger dimensions, then enlarge upstream/top or move outlet independently if response changes materially. Measure the same physical metric/probes, difference of the two cases in each domain, and its uncertainty. Require the absolute change between effects including sampling uncertainty to be smaller than a prespecified fraction of the decision margin (e.g. one third), and each plausible effect keeps its sign. A single retracted larger run cannot establish this. If larger but still confined run differs or reference remains disturbed, keep enlarging coarsely before paying for local refinement or label result as a confined rig. One might initially try several nose heights (e.g. 3–5) upstream and several body heights behind a joined tail, then verify with a further enlargement. Downstream plane must be beyond recirculation and in a slowly varying outflow; "3–5" is an engineering starting point only.
- For the body that intersects the outlet, specifying the same static pressure above and beneath it imposes a particular zero end-pressure difference. Channel length and pressure drop set underfloor flow and can influence lip pressure strongly. Use a coarser credible parent section to prescribe or bracket the pressure difference between branches/one branch impedance while allowing flow to respond; vary the cut-plane, resistance and parent data. A common downstream pressure is a valid *rig* assumption when explicitly chosen, but has to survive the same sensitivity to support a car-related qualitative sign. Never fix pressure and normal flux independently on the same section. A finite section needs a measured or plausible continuous taper and enough wake, not an artificial sudden expansion close to the lip.
- Cost estimate at the brief's 16M updates/s: one original-size transit is 520×500×5200/16M≈85 s coarse and 1040×1000×10400/16M≈676 s fine, per state before overhead. Doubling height and longitudinal length at fixed h multiplies cost per transit by 8. A 10 m by 8 m box at 2.5 mm and U=0.1 costs ≈8.9 h per transit at that rate. A larger uniform full-car fine-grid study can exhaust the budget in a single transit. Use coarse full-body or calibrated local section, restart/spin up fine only after boundary and metric tests on both coarse cases, then allocate two fine states only if decisive. If sensitivity/independence cannot be demonstrated, keep the illustration qualitative.

### Gaps

- There is no known exterior pressure/flow split from a validated parent or experiment for the split outlet, and no completed paired extended-domain splitter results at inspection. Absolute sizes for a supplied effect tolerable to the museum cannot be calculated without runs.

## What does the present pressure probe actually measure?

### Takeaway
It measures two fluid boxes in both cases, but only the forward case puts material between them. The reported +0.74 to +0.99 sign cannot support a comparative pressure load on the lip under any convergence rule.

### Cited Findings

- The retracted lip's leading edge is x=935 mm and forward edge x=975 mm, with x increasing toward the nose. Both pressure boxes cover x=940–970 mm. For retracted geometry these are entirely ahead of the bumper in free fluid. For forward geometry they lie above and below the extension, averaging fluid over 16–18 mm vertical extent rather than wall pressure. [Exact edges, polygons and sampling](../../tools/cfd/part.mjs#L94)
- Pressure acts normal to solid surfaces, and uniform pressure sums to zero over a closed surface. [NASA pressure force integral](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/aerodynamic-force/)

### Inferences

- A pressure load per span with downward positive on an actual horizontal exposed strip is `F′down,p=∫(ptop−pbottom)dx`; dividing by common fixed `q_ref c_ref` gives a comparable dimensionless coefficient. For curved faces, integrate `∫(p−p_ref)n_z ds` over a properly defined wetted contour, outward normal to solid, z upward. On a complete closed section a common additive offset cancels. Keep pressure and full traction separate; local wall momentum-exchange forces cross-check the vertical integral and include viscosity. An average over fixed physical interval is a mean pressure jump only if both faces exist throughout; its extent gives the force on that patch, not vehicle downforce. If measuring exposed added area only, its contribution is zero by definition in the flush setting, never an open-fluid pseudo-load. Better two-state whole-forebody comparison: integrate true vertical traction over actual matched upper and lower contour through the same downstream body station in each case, ensuring uniform gauge cancels, and compare the net change. Compare extrapolated wall pressure against a second wall-evaluation distance and the momentum exchange; thin lip leading corners need enough resolution.
- Current guide can legitimately depict the two model geometries and how the *calculated flow* divides around them, labelled as a developing slice. No splitter load sign should appear. The car file itself labels the 40 mm travel and 25 mm thickness illustrative and estimates the body/nose trace uncertainty at about ±10 cm. [Case caveats](../../stops/190e-evo2/car.js#L129)

### Gaps

- This geometry has no exposed upper face over a common projected interval when flush. It lacks independent measurements of travel/shape at the lip scale and has no computed tagged lip/forebody force trace.
