## Evolution II — give the racing engine one short experiment

**Yes, the 2.5-litre four is worth a second step.** It earns its place by showing how little time a racing cylinder has to fill. Mercedes-Benz specifies a 97.8 mm bore and 82.8 mm stroke for the race engine. The early DTM version produced 245 kW at 8,500 rpm and could reach 9,500 rpm. The figure quoted for the 1992 championship car is **up to 274 kW (373 PS)**. The road Evo II had a 97.3 mm bore and produced 173 kW (235 PS). Keep each power figure attached to its version. The source gives the early engine’s maximum speed separately from the 1992 power figure. [Mercedes-Benz on the road and race engines](https://media.mercedes-benz.com/en/article/25dd00e4-5ef9-4c2f-b12c-55169f795d4d)

Ask **“At 9,000 rpm, how long does one cylinder have for its intake stroke?”** / **„Percenként 9000-es fordulatszámnál hány ezredmásodpercig tart a szívóütem?”** Let the visitor switch between 4,500 and 9,000 rpm while watching one cylinder in slow motion. The crank turns through the intake stroke in **6.7 ms and 3.3 ms** respectively. Highlight the two inlet valves opening together as the piston descends. Freeze the view from above so the visitor can see both inlet openings available to fill the cylinder in that brief interval. In the side view, the short stroke means the piston travels less distance per revolution at a given engine speed. Put the real elapsed time beside the picture. These times describe 180° of crank rotation. Actual valve opening extends over a different interval determined by cam timing.

Reuse the four-stroke animation and show **two camshafts and four valves per cylinder** before inviting visitors to count them. The current generic DOHC view draws one inlet and one exhaust valve per cylinder. The FIA head photograph on sheet 7 gives the model a visual reference. Use a slow-motion time scale and a pause control so visitors can follow one cam revolution through two crank revolutions. Put power figures in the supporting text. The useful result of the experiment is the time available to breathe. ## Straight-sixes and straight-eights — why a smooth engine can still twist its crankshaft

Give the **300 SL** the balance experiment, the **770** the firing-pulse comparison and the **W 196 R** the historical engineering decision. The moving pistons exert reactions on the engine block and its mounts. Within each cylinder, the primary inertia force repeats once per crank revolution. The connecting rod’s changing angle introduces a secondary component that repeats twice. In the tour’s symmetric, equal-mass six- and eight-cylinder layouts, the first- and second-order forces and their rocking moments cancel in the sum. The crankshaft still receives torque pulses at different positions along its length, and the driveline carries the resulting fluctuating torque. [Engine kinematics](../lib/model/engine.js) · [Reciprocating forces and engine balance](https://www.epi-eng.com/piston_engine_technology/forces_on_recip_components.htm)

| Stop | What the visitor does and sees | Discussion prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **300 SL Gullwing · Straight-six balance** | Pause the existing engine and step through a revolution. Show a force arrow along each cylinder, then their sum and the rocking moment about the engine midpoint. Pistons 1 and 6 move together. Add the other two pairs and watch the total cancel. Switch between the once-per-revolution and twice-per-revolution components. The 190 E’s conventional inline-four makes a useful comparison because its secondary forces add. | “How can six moving pistons leave the engine balanced?” / „Hogyan egyenlítik ki egymás tehetetlenségi erőit a soros hathengeres dugattyúi?” |
| **770 · Straight-eight firing pulses** | Keep the existing firing animation and put a torque trace at the crank output. At the same 3,000 rpm, an evenly firing four-stroke six has three firing pulses per revolution (150 per second), and an evenly firing eight has four (200 per second). Highlight each firing cylinder as its pulse reaches the trace. Compare at equal mean torque so visitors can distinguish pulse spacing from engine power. | “Can you still pick out the individual power strokes at the output?” / „A főtengelyen leadott nyomaték görbéjén felismerhetők az egyes munkaütemek?” |
| **W 196 R · Crankshaft resonance** | After the aero story, show the two groups of four cylinders and the gear that takes power from the middle of the crankshaft. Mark the front, centre and rear of the shaft with witness lines. Sweep engine rpm and watch the relative twist grow near a torsional natural frequency. Let visitors compare the actual centre take-off with an illustrative end take-off under the same firing input and load. Trace the torque path to the propeller shaft and rear transaxle. | “Why did Mercedes take the drive from the middle of the crankshaft?” / „Miért a főtengely közepéről hajtották meg a hátsó kerekeket?” |

The W 196 R makes the distinction tangible. Taking power from the middle gives each four-cylinder group a shorter path to the output gear. Mercedes-Benz records vibration as a reason for that choice. Resonance is a separate observation in the model: when a repeated torque input excites a natural twisting mode, the relative angle can grow even while the engine remains externally balanced. Offer a damping control to see the resonance peak diminish. Label it as a model experiment. [300 SL engine](../stops/300sl/car.js) · [770 engine](../stops/770/car.js) · [Mercedes-Benz archive on the M 196 centre drive](https://mercedes-benz-publicarchive.com/marsClassic/en/instance/ko/Mercedes-Benz-W-196-R-streamlined-racing-car-1954.xhtml?oid=6873) · [Measured six-cylinder crankshaft response with and without a damper](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/experiment-computation-analyses-torsional-vibration-crankshaft-system-viscous-torsional-damper-diesel-engine-1999-01-1748)

**Model and proof.** Reuse the existing crank angles, cylinder positions, stroke and rod length for the balance arrows. Use real rpm for the calculations and slow motion for the picture. For torsion, connect front, centre and rear crank inertias with elastic, damped segments and couple the chosen output to a driveline load.
