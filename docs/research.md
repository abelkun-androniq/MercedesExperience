# Source Document: Mercedes-Benz Museum, Stuttgart - Vehicle & Exhibit Database

## Part 1: Comprehensive List of Displayed Cars (Highlights)
The Mercedes-Benz Museum spans nine floors and 16,500 square meters, housing a total of 160 vehicles and over 1,500 exhibits. While a complete manifest of all 160 vehicles is subject to rotation, the permanent exhibition is structured into 7 chronological "Legend Rooms" and 5 thematic "Collection Rooms". Below is the comprehensive list of the most prominent vehicles on display, organized by their respective exhibit areas:

### Legend 1: Pioneers – The Invention of the Automobile (1886–1900)
*   **Daimler Single-Cylinder "Grandfather Clock" Engine (1884):** A structural sculpture of steel and brass that heralded modern mobility.
*   **Daimler Riding Carriage / Reitwagen (1885):** The world's first motorcycle.
*   **Benz Patent-Motorwagen (1886):** Recognized as the first functional car powered by a combustion engine.
*   **Daimler Motorboat "Marie" (1888):** A prominent early application of the combustion engine for water travel. 
*   **Benz Victoria (1893):** An early endurance vehicle that tackled a 939-kilometer journey.
*   **Benz Motor Velocipede (1894):** Regarded as the first large-scale production car.
*   **Daimler Motorised Business Vehicle (1899):** An early commercial truck capable of a 500 kg payload.

### Legend 2 & 3: Birth of the Brand & Times of Change (1900–1945)
*   **Mercedes-Simplex 40 PS (1902):** Often referred to as the oldest Mercedes still in existence and the first automobile of the modern age.
*   **Milnes-Daimler Double-Decker Bus (1904):** An early transit vehicle used in London.
*   **Benz 200 PS "Lightning Benz" / "Blitzen Benz" (1909):** A massive 1,312 cu in displacement racer capable of 141 mph.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 27/170 Typ SSK (1928):** A legendary pre-war open-wheel sports car with an angled front grille.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 540 K Streamliner (1938):** A masterclass in pre-war aerodynamic aesthetics.
*   **Mercedes-Benz L6500 Pritschenwagen (1938):** A heavy-duty pre-war industrial cargo truck.

### Legend 4 & 5: Post-war Miracle & Visionaries (1945–1982)
*   **Mercedes-Benz 300 SL "Gullwing" (1954):** The iconic dream car of the 1950s with upward-opening doors.
*   **Mercedes-Benz Rennwagen-Schnelltransporter "Blue Wonder" (1955):** A high-speed flat-bed transport truck for race cars.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 300 SLR Uhlenhaut Coupe (1955):** A brutally fast roadster built from race car mechanics.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 190 SL (1955):** A beautiful classic roadster.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 220 S "Tailfin" (1959):** Known for its North American-inspired rear directional wings.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 300 SEL 6.3 (1968):** The top-of-the-line luxury predecessor to the modern S-Class.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 230 G "Papamobil" (1980):** The iconic modified G-Wagen used by Pope John Paul II.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 500 SLC Rallyewagen (1980):** A rare long-distance rally racer.

### Legend 6, Legend 7 & Collection Rooms
*   **Mercedes-Benz Vision R 320 CDI (2005):** A concept vehicle showcasing emission-free mobility ambitions.
*   **Mercedes-Maybach 6 Concept:** A modern, dramatic luxury showcase.
*   **Mercedes-Benz C 111-II:** An experimental concept car.
*   **Mercedes-Benz 1624 Car Transporter:** A massive multi-level commercial vehicle transporter positioned high up in Collection Room 2.
*   **Silver Arrows Racing Cars:** Found in Legend 7 on an expansive banked curve display, celebrating the brand's racing lineage.

## Part 2: Vehicles Exhibited for "Close-Up" Inspection
The museum has a specifically curated "Close-up" series, which highlights specific exhibits that allow visitors to see mechanical elements, raw craftsmanship, and intricate detailing at close range. These exhibits are specifically designed to expose their inner workings:

1.  **Daimler Riding Carriage (1885):** Visitors can view the exposed two simple control levers (a metal handle for mixture formation and a wooden knob for the block brake), the glow-tube ignition tank, and the leather drive belts.
2.  **Daimler 'Grandfather Clock' Engine (1884):** Displayed as a bare steel and brass sculpture so the vertical cylinder and round crankcase can be closely examined.
3.  **Benz Victoria (1893):** Positioned so the mechanical geometry of its early kingpin steering linkage can be seen.
4.  **Daimler Motorboat 'Marie' (1888):** Visitors can inspect the white-painted doors that discreetly concealed the single-cylinder engine from passengers.
5.  **Daimler Motorised Business Vehicle (1899):** The sieve-like front panel is exposed to show the tubular radiator designed by Wilhelm Maybach.
6.  **Mercedes-Benz 1624 Car Transporter:** Exhibited so the heavy-duty hydraulic control elements and the hybrid drive of the loading platforms (pulley blocks and hydraulics) can be inspected in high detail.

## Part 3: Technical Highlights & Corresponding Cars (Worthy of Separate Discussion)
This structured list isolates the most important technical innovations present at the museum. It maps directly to specific vehicles and serves as an excellent foundation for a NotebookLM study guide, visual presentation, or isolated audio discussion.

*   **First High-Speed Four-Stroke Engine**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle/Exhibit:** Daimler "Grandfather Clock" Engine (1884)
    *   **Technical Details:** Developed in a garden house by Gottlieb Daimler and Wilhelm Maybach. Initially produced 1 hp (0.5 hp in the riding carriage) at a then-astounding 600 revolutions per minute.
*   **Kingpin Steering Innovation**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Benz Victoria (1893)
    *   **Technical Details:** Replaced crude central-pivot steering systems, making cornering noticeably safer and allowing vehicles to comfortably navigate nearly 1,000 km journeys on primitive roads.
*   **Invention of the Tubular Radiator**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Daimler Motorised Business Vehicle (1899)
    *   **Technical Details:** Created by Wilhelm Maybach to drastically improve engine cooling efficiency under loads, which enabled commercial vehicles to securely carry payloads up to 500 kg at 16 km/h.
*   **Supercharged Hillclimb Engineering**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Mercedes-Benz 27/170 Typ SSK (1928)
    *   **Technical Details:** Featured a massive 220-hp supercharged inline-six engine housed in a skinny, lightweight fuselage built specifically for dominance in uphill racing.
*   **Early Implementation of Modern Aerodynamics**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Mercedes-Benz 540 K Streamliner (1938)
    *   **Technical Details:** Despite featuring free-standing front mudguards, it utilized sweeping, wind-tunnel-tested curves to achieve a highly impressive drag coefficient (Cd) of just 0.36. 
*   **High-Torque Heavy Diesel Powertrain**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Mercedes-Benz L6500 Pritschenwagen (1938)
    *   **Technical Details:** Equipped with a whopping 12.5-liter six-cylinder diesel engine positioned in front of the forward axle, enabling the transport of up to 14,000 pounds of payload.
*   **Bridging Race-Car Physics with Road-Car Usability**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Mercedes-Benz 300 SLR Uhlenhaut Coupe (1955)
    *   **Technical Details:** Housed the mechanical chassis and a 3.0-liter inline-eight engine (310 hp) from a pure race car. Capable of an astonishing 180 mph in an era where most passenger cars struggled to hit 100 mph.
*   **High-Speed Commercial Aerodynamics**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Mercedes-Benz Rennwagen-Schnelltransporter (1955)
    *   **Technical Details:** A heavy transport flat-bed utilizing a slippery aerodynamic profile and a 3.0-liter inline-six engine (approx. 200 hp), enabling a loaded truck to cruise on the Autobahn at 106 mph.
*   **Integrated Projectile Armor on a Passenger Chassis**
    *   **Corresponding Vehicle:** Mercedes-Benz 230 G "Papamobil" (1980)
    *   **Technical Details:** A standard luxurious G-Wagen chassis that was heavily modified with golden adornments, a high-visibility seating cupola, and later retrofitted with comprehensive projectile protection in 1981.
